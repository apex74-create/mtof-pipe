## A. WHAT THE MTOF IS

**Sources:** scope package §§1–3 and §6; comparative board A–D; daily record, 16:19–16:32 EST. **Grades:** name and author's mechanics are doctrine of record; wrapper and adapters are AMBER design; bucket and PE components carry the separate grades in Section C.

1. **The author's one-line definition — BLUE:** the MTOF is **“an enriched JSON format, essentially.”** Plain JSON is the alphabet: keys, values, arrays, and nesting, with no clock, no internal authority, no carrying discipline, and no endpoint contract. The MTOF is the model built on that alphabet: wrapper, bucket discipline, PE timing, coin proof, and endpoint adapters.
2. **Why “Multiplex” — BLUE, author's rationale:** “each one of those plexes or routes can have multiple faces… it's really a multi-dimensional situation… **multi-face, multi-dimensional, multi-layer transformation**.” A plex is a route; a route can present many faces; the assembly is multi-dimensional and multi-layer in its transformations.
3. **The MTOF loop — BLUE, author's plain mechanics:** **convert to tokens → cram → fly it across the gap → unconvert at the far end into whatever layer the destination needs.** In his words: “We're converting to tokens, cramming a shitload in the same space, and flying it across the gap, and then unconverting into whatever layer five we need it to be.”
4. **The wrapper — AMBER:** a multi-dimensional JSON wrapper carries the payload, the bucket manifest, the PE timing header, the coin proof, and the face being presented. The first-draft shape is in scope package §6.2. It is designed, not yet a ratified wire standard.
5. **The bucket — GREEN as a discipline, AMBER inside this wrapper:** the payload is split into sealed buckets under one manifest. The Nov 14 house bench measured the bucket discipline on real records; the Nov 15 test bed then exercised the discipline inside an MTOF string. The two measurements are kept separate in Section C.
6. **The clock — AMBER as universal design, GREEN in its measured pieces:** PE timing is the shared clock every endpoint can compute without negotiating a session. Its formula of record is **PE(t) = sin(2π × (t mod C) / C)**. The library firmware carries **CYCLE 11000 ms** and **BASELINE 369 ms**. The owner's universal-clock claim — PE timing used through the whole assembly so it “comes together like a clock” — remains a design claim until more benches measure it in each new seat.
7. **The coin — AMBER / doctrine:** the coin rides inside the assembly as authentication and traversal proof. The written edge is the seven-dimension pre-image in the ADP draft's Claim 5 and the implemented `mint5DCoin` in the library copy. Past that written edge, the owner designated the coin logic **“not written but understood.”** Section G preserves the six-versus-seven-dimension discrepancy.
8. **The adapters — AMBER:** endpoint adapters convert the string back to the transport of agnostic choice. The string does not dictate the pipe. The adapter knows the band, licence, substrate, and law it is standing in, and decides what may lawfully ride that leg.
9. **THE HEADER IS THE LAYER — BLUE / named principle, added 16:58 EST:** neither pipe takes the other to go. The access-point data layer already exists; Apex is making **its** layer. One side is **observation**; the other is the **rich transport** — observation controller / rich transport. The MTOF layer needs neither substrate exclusively **because it's all in the header:** identity, proof, timing, routing, and enrichment level ride in the header, making every string self-describing and self-proving. Observation and control can read the header without opening the payload; transport carries the string over whichever pipe the groove allows. **The layer floats above both pipes, owns neither, and is owned by neither.**
10. **Discernible enrichment — AMBER:** under licence, the receiving side can discern the level of enrichment that actually dropped back through the JSON opening. Enrichment is therefore treated as a quantity to be read at arrival, not merely claimed at departure. No remote-node measurement of enrichment exists yet.
11. **Recognition — AMBER spec fragment:** the security-layer fragment of record calls for a recognition authorization protocol for browser input sessions that are **gated and maintained but not slow**, with everything below the spoken **400** line — milliseconds / megahertz as spoken — the “super zone” in which the 369 ms PE baseline already sits. The principle is recognition over interrogation: the session is recognized, not endlessly re-challenged. No recognition-layer bench is in this package.
12. **The lock doctrine — BLUE:** “I want my lock back.” Security by two parties merely asserting “we're secure” is rejected. The house standard is a mechanism the user holds — a lock, a key, a gate he controls and can verify. This is distinct from the money layer's handshake-across-the-hood standard in Section H: the hood handshake is verified by the idle you can watch; the web security handshake too often asks you to believe.

## B. THE LAW — THE LICENCE IS THE GUIDEWAY

**Sources:** scope package §4; cram pack Clusters 1 and 5 and §P; test bed RESULTS §4; daily record, 15:32 and 16:46–16:54 EST. **Grade:** BLUE standing information and scoped shape-checking. **This section is not legal advice.** The Legal lane confirms before any real transmission.

1. **The owner's synthesis — BLUE:** **Part 97 is not a transport layer at all. It is a licence.** The transport can be built; what Part 97 governs is who may transmit, for what purpose, under whose responsibility, and in what shape. In his formulation, **whatever licensing intervention applies guides the development structure — the guideway path.**
2. **Part 97's purpose — BLUE:** §97.1 states the Amateur Radio Service's principles: a voluntary noncommercial communication service, particularly for emergency communications; advancement of the radio art; advancement of communication and technical skills; a reservoir of trained operators, technicians, and electronics experts; and international goodwill.
3. **The amateur definition — BLUE:** §97.3 defines the amateur service as self-training, intercommunication, and technical investigation by duly authorized persons interested in radio technique **“solely with a personal aim and without pecuniary interest.”**
4. **The commercial bar — BLUE:** §97.113(a)(3) bars communications in which the station licensee or control operator has a pecuniary interest, including communications on behalf of an employer, subject to the rule's stated exceptions. A paid product's business traffic therefore does not become lawful on ham merely because its format is clever.
5. **The obscuring bar — BLUE:** §97.113(a)(4) bars, among other things, messages encoded for the purpose of obscuring their meaning, except as otherwise provided. §97.309 repeats the no-obscuring rule for digital codes. Encryption of the payload to conceal its meaning is not the Part 97 groove.
6. **The digital-code door that does exist — BLUE:** §97.309(a) names specified codes and permits a technique whose technical characteristics have been documented publicly. §97.309(b) also addresses unspecified digital codes, with the standing conditions that they must not obscure meaning and that, when required by the FCC, the station must be able to produce a record convertible to the original information. The MTOF's scoped ham shape is therefore a **published format, a readable payload, and a coin used as authentication — not concealment.**
7. **Authentication is not concealment — BLUE / scoped posture:** the coin in the test bed is a SHA-256 proof recomputed from seven public dimensions carried in the clear. It verifies origin and traversal; the payload beside it remains readable. The scope package treats this authentication posture as Part 97-shaped. It is a scoped design posture, not a court ruling.
8. **Control and identification — BLUE:** a licensed control operator answers for the station's transmissions. Station identification follows §97.119 — in the cram pack's exam form, the call sign is given at least every 10 minutes during a communication and at its end. Section F turns this into the network rule: legality is held hop by hop by named licensees.
9. **Power — BLUE:** §97.313(a) requires the minimum transmitter power necessary to carry out the desired communications. §97.313(b) states the general ceiling of **1.5 kW PEP**, with narrower limits in particular bands and classes. The cram pack carries the Technician HF limit of **200 W PEP** where it applies.
10. **The narrow exceptions are not data doors — BLUE:** telecommand of a space station and of radio-controlled model craft are the scoped exceptions named in the record. They are control-signal carve-outs. They do not authorize a general encrypted data service.
11. **Part 95 contrast — BLUE:** Part 95 covers the Personal Radio Services — CB, GMRS, FRS, and MURS in the scoped record. The scoped pass found **no obscured-message groove**: §95.1733(a)(3) bars coded messages or messages with hidden meanings in its Part 95 context; CB carries plain-language rules; and the equipment-certification regime is itself a bar to obscuring capability, including §95.381 for MURS in the scope package. Part 95 is not a back door for the encrypted bucket.
12. **The grooves that do exist — BLUE:**
    1. **Part 97:** readable, published, authenticated, noncommercial traffic by licensed amateurs.
    2. **Part 15 ISM:** lawful encryption in the scoped record, unlicensed but power- and equipment-limited; the Meshtastic-class mesh precedent lives here.
    3. **Part 90 Land Mobile:** licensed commercial land-mobile groove where encryption is lawful in the scoped record.
    4. **IP transports:** ordinary addressed internet, wired, and private-network transports, where ordinary payload encryption is not governed by the Part 97 obscuring bar.
13. **The adapter decides — BLUE design consequence:** the same MTOF string may fly readable and authenticated on a Part 97 leg, and encrypted on a Part 15, Part 90, or IP leg. **The adapter, not the string, decides encryption, because the adapter knows which groove it is standing in.**
14. **The transport's moral structure — BLUE, owner's naming:** Part 97's shape — published, readable, noncommercial, authenticated, and never obscured — is not merely a constraint. The owner named it **“our moral and ethics structure for our transport layer,”** built for “disenfranchised, distant, last-mile representative relatives.” His endpoint catalogue stands in his words: “in the bush… on the plateau, in a tree, on a mountaintop, in a monk repository, whatever, man.” The reach follows from the openness: nothing hidden, nothing being sold on that leg, and any endpoint able to read and verify what arrived.
